> Abhimanyu always chooses the harder way — to him, the shorter way is one everyone else has already taken.

The older I get, the more I notice how much of where I stand is luck: the family I was born into, the country, the decade, the body that has mostly worked. I worked for the rest. But the starting line was handed to me.

That's the thought this note exists for. If a good part of my position is unearned, the honest response isn't guilt and it isn't a donation — it's taking on the risk I can afford to take. Working on the problems that are genuinely hard, staying with them longer than is comfortable, and opening doors for people I meet along the way.

I read somewhere *"the privileged must risk it all, over and over, until they are tortured to greatness."* I believe the first half. The second half is theatrical: suffering isn't the goal, and nobody is owed my martyrdom. What I actually want to do is smaller and more useful — **redistribute risk**. I can absorb a bad bet that would end someone else's run, so I should be the one making it.

That's my version of socialism, and it's the version I can practise without asking anyone's permission first.
