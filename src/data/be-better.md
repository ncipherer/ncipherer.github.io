For years, I quietly feared I may become a bad person later in life. I thought being good meant not doing anything wrong — being polite, staying out of trouble, never hurting anyone. Then I met that stranger. He was kind, honest, well-liked. And completely useless when someone was dying in front of him. I had confused being harmless with being good.

Everyone around me is bleeding somewhere. They need a roof, a meal, a quiet hour, a person who stays. And I keep rediscovering that being inoffensive was never the job. Doing something for someone that they could not do for themselves — that is the job. A kindness that never reaches the wound is just a nice thing said while somebody else does the work.

This note isn't a case I'm building against myself. Because good is not a verdict you get to rest in; it isn't something you finish. It's something you keep doing, again and again. I keep this list because I want to become someone who can actually help — not just someone who means well. One entry at a time.

A second order effect of writing this list has been I realised how much I've changed - my personality, opinions, things I once believed completely. Sometimes it happened slowly with experience. Other times someone who cared about me literally fought me on things. I keep adding to it as I notice new things. There's no finished version of this.

It has taken me a long time to realize that a lot of the things I am tracking here — the sharp edges, the old reactions, the mistakes — started long before I knew any better. A lot of the behaviors or beliefs I regret are just defense mechanisms or patterns built by the situation around me. The person I was then is not the person I am now, and I'm slowly accepting that I don't have to keep proving myself to the person I was back then.

> "In any situation, you're not responsible for your first thought. Your conditioning is. But, your second and third thoughts? Those are entirely up to you."

This note is a record of me overriding my automatic conditioning. The old habits might be what my environment handed me, but the work I am doing right here is me choosing my second and third thoughts.

## Righteous

I had strong opinions about how things should be done. Built standards around what worked for me, then applied them to everyone. Ran them on myself just as hard — moved the bar every time I got close.

I still believe in standards. I just don't carry them as a verdict anymore. I can be okay with where any person—including myself—is right now.

## Empathy
Strangers were easy. The people closest to me were not. That's where I ran out of patience. Warmth to people who can't affect me isn't empathy — it's easy kindness. The real test was how I treated people who knew me well. I made a deliberate effort: slow down, ask first, make room. It's no longer deliberate. It's how I am now.

## Fighting
Disagreeing right takes courage and empathy. Courage to ask if I'm the volatile one. Empathy to find the other person's negotiables and non-negotiables. Staying connected during a disagreement is the whole point — not resolving it cleanly. I've learned to convey my feelings, and to take a break when flooded, because that break is where I reconnect.

## Patience
I wanted to understand the hardest version first before committing. Fine for skills. Nonsense for people. Without visible progress I'd reach for reassurance. Constant check-ins. If it didn't come, anxiety filled the gaps. Wanting to know where you stand isn't the problem. How I responded was. I started treating patience as something to pick up and use. I've gotten better at trusting what's already there.

## Maintenance
With someone, I'm fully there. In between? I used to go quiet. No texts, no calls. I said that's how I'm wired — depth over frequency. Truth in that. But also a convenient excuse. A relationship I don't feed isn't a relationship — it's a memory. I feed the ones that matter now.

## Silence
My wins I share immediately. My problems I used to keep to myself. The people I love were the one space I kept separate. But underneath: they already have enough on their plate. Mine doesn't count. They kept asking me to share because they wanted to hear it. The excuse fell apart. I wasn't protecting them. I was deciding on their behalf that they couldn't hold it.

Keeping silent was my first thought. Learning to speak up and trust was my second. Letting them in isn't a burden. It's a gift I was withholding.
