# Soya Chunks
### Prep: 10 mins | Cook: 20 mins

## Ingredients
- 100g Soya Chunks
- 1 Onion, chopped
- 1 Tomato, chopped
- 1 tbsp Oil
- Optional: 1 tsp Ginger-Garlic paste
- Optional: Juice of half a lemon
- Spices: Salt, Turmeric, Red chili powder, Garam Masala

## Instructions
1. **Boil the Soya** - *5-7 mins* - Put the soya chunks in enough water to submerge them fully. Add a pinch of salt and turmeric. Boil at 180°C until a thick white foam rises to the top and the chunks are soft when pressed.
2. **Rinse and Squeeze** - *5 mins* - Strain the hot water. Run cold water over the chunks. Squeeze out all the absorbed water thoroughly using your hands or a cloth — this step is important for texture.
3. **Fry Aromatics** - *4-5 mins* - Heat oil in a pan at 130°C. Add chopped onions and fry until soft and translucent. Add ginger-garlic paste (if using) and cook for 1 more minute before adding the chopped tomato. Cook until the tomato is completely mushy.
4. **Add Spices** - *2 mins* - Stir in the turmeric, red chili powder, garam masala, and salt directly into the onion-tomato base. Mix well so the spices coat everything evenly.
5. **Mix and Finish** - *3-4 mins* - Add the squeezed soya chunks into the pan. Stir everything together at 100°C for 3 to 4 minutes until the chunks are well coated and lightly browned. Finish with a squeeze of lemon juice if desired.


# Chole
### Prep: 6-8 hours | Cook: 15 mins

## Ingredients
- 1 cup White Chickpeas (Chole), dry
- 2 cups Water (for pressure cooking)
- 1 Onion, chopped
- 1 Tomato, chopped
- 1 tbsp Ghee or Oil
- Spices: Salt, Dhaniya powder, Red chili powder, Haldi, Garam Masala, Kasuri Methi, Jeera powder

## Instructions
1. **Soak and Prep** - *6-8 hours* - Soak 1 cup of dry chole in plenty of water for 6 to 8 hours or overnight. Discard the soaking water and rinse the chole thoroughly with fresh water before cooking.
2. **Pressure Cook** - *6 mins* - Put the soaked chole into the pressure cooker. Add 2 cups of fresh water, a pinch of salt, the chopped onion, and the chopped tomato. Pressure cook at 180°C for 8 minutes.
3. **Stir Fry in the Cooker** - *5 mins* - Open the cooker once the pressure is fully released. Keep the chole inside. Add the ghee or oil, dhaniya powder, red chili powder, haldi, and salt directly into the cooker. Stir and fry everything together on 130°C for 5 minutes — the chole will absorb the spices and the base will thicken nicely.
4. **Finish for Flavor** - *1-2 mins* - Turn off the heat. Stir in the garam masala, kasuri methi, and jeera powder at the very end so they retain their aroma.


# Cooked Rice
### Prep: 2-3 mins | Cook: 22 mins

## Ingredients
- 2 cups Rice (White or Brown)
- 4 cups Water

## Instructions
1. **Wash the Rice** - *2-3 mins* - Wash 2 cups of rice thoroughly with fresh water 2 to 3 times until the water runs mostly clear.
2. **Pressure Cook** - *6-7 mins (White) or 7 mins (Brown)* - Put the washed rice and 4 cups of water into the pressure cooker. For White Rice: pressure cook at 180°C for 6 to 7 minutes, then turn off the heat. For Brown Rice: cook at 180°C for the first 4 minutes, then lower to 130°C for the remaining 3 minutes. Note: Both varieties use the same 1:2 rice-to-water ratio.
3. **Release Pressure** - *10-15 mins* - Turn off the heat and wait 10 to 15 minutes until the pressure is fully and naturally released before opening the cooker.


# Dal
### Prep: 5 mins | Cook: 25 mins

## Ingredients
- 1 cup Dal (Toor, Masoor, or Moong — all work, though cook times may vary slightly)
- 3 cups Water
- 1 Onion, chopped
- 1 Tomato, chopped
- 1 tbsp Oil or Ghee
- Spices: Jeera (cumin seeds), Turmeric, Salt, Garam Masala, Kasuri Methi

## Instructions
1. **Wash the Dal** - *2-3 mins* - Rinse 1 cup of dal thoroughly with fresh water before you start cooking to remove excess starch and any debris.
2. **Fry Aromatics** - *3 mins* - Put oil in the pressure cooker and heat it at 130°C. Add the jeera, chopped onion, and chopped tomato. Fry them together for 3 minutes until the onion softens.
3. **Add Dal and Spices** - *2 mins* - Add the washed dal, turmeric, and salt into the cooker. Stir and fry everything together for about 2 minutes so the spices coat the dal.
4. **Pressure Cook** - *6-7 mins* - Pour 3 cups of water into the cooker. Close the lid and pressure cook at 180°C for 6 to 7 minutes.
5. **Finish and Flavor** - *10-15 mins* - Turn off the heat and let the cooker release pressure naturally — this takes about 10 to 15 minutes. Open the lid, then stir in kasuri methi, a little garam masala, and a splash of ghee for richness.


# Poha
### Prep: 5-10 mins | Cook: 8 mins

## Ingredients
- 3 cups Poha (Flattened rice)
- 3 tbsp Groundnuts (Peanuts)
- 1 Onion, chopped (plus extra raw onion for serving)
- 1 Tomato, chopped (for serving)
- 1 tbsp Oil
- Namkeen (for serving)
- Spices: Jeera (cumin seeds), Haldi (turmeric), Red chili powder, Dry mango powder (amchur), Salt, Kasuri Methi

## Instructions
1. **Wash and Soak** - *5-10 mins* - Wash 3 cups of poha thoroughly with water. Drain completely, cover, and leave to soak for 5 to 10 minutes until it is soft and pliable but not mushy.
2. **Fry Groundnuts and Aromatics** - *3-4 mins* - Heat oil in a frying pan at 200°C. Add the groundnuts first and fry until crunchy. Then add the jeera and chopped onion to the same oil. Fry until the onion turns golden — not brown or dark, as that will make it bitter.
3. **Bloom the Spices** - *1 min* - Add haldi, red chili powder, and dry mango powder directly into the pan. Stir quickly for about 30 seconds to bloom the spices in the oil.
4. **Mix and Finish** - *2-3 mins* - Add the soaked poha into the pan. Add salt and kasuri methi. Stir everything together gently at 100°C until the poha is evenly yellow and heated through.
5. **Serve** - *1 min* - Top with fresh raw chopped onion, chopped tomato, and a side of crunchy namkeen.


# Suji Ka Halwa
### Prep: 2 mins | Cook: 16 mins

## Ingredients
- 1.5 cups Suji (Semolina)
- 2 tbsp Ghee
- 6 tbsp Jaggery (Gur), roughly broken into small pieces
- Dry fruits of your choice (cashews, raisins, almonds), roughly chopped
- Water (enough to fully submerge the suji — approximately 3 cups)

## Instructions
1. **Roast the Suji** - *6 mins* - Dry roast 1.5 cups of suji in a pan at 220°C for 6 minutes, stirring continuously, until it turns a light golden brown and smells nutty.
2. **Add Ghee** - *4 mins* - Add 2 tbsp of ghee to the roasted suji. Stir and fry everything together at 130°C for 4 minutes until the ghee is fully absorbed and the suji is evenly coated.
3. **Add Dry Fruits** - *1 min* - Add your roughly chopped dry fruits into the pan and stir them in. Note: To easily slice harder dry fruits like cashews, you can use a vegetable slicer with a splash of water to keep them from slipping.
4. **Sweeten and Simmer** - *5-6 mins* - Add 6 tbsp of jaggery and enough water to fully submerge the suji (around 3 cups). Stir continuously at 100°C until almost all the water is absorbed and the halwa comes together. Note: It is better to add more water than less — too little water will leave the halwa dry and grainy.


# Bhindi Ki Sabzi
### Prep: 10 mins | Cook: 13 mins

## Ingredients
- 250g Bhindi (Okra), chopped into 1-inch pieces
- 1 Onion, chopped
- 1 Tomato, chopped (divided — a small bit for early, rest for the end)
- 1 tbsp Oil
- Spices: Jeera (cumin seeds), Salt, Haldi (turmeric), Coriander powder, Red chili powder

## Instructions
1. **Wash and Dry Bhindi** - *10 mins* - Wash the bhindi thoroughly and spread them out to dry completely for at least 10 minutes before chopping. Note: Cutting wet bhindi causes the sliminess (lacing) that makes it sticky and unpleasant — fully dry bhindi cooks clean.
2. **Sauté Aromatics** - *2-3 mins* - Heat oil in a pan at 130°C. Add the jeera, chopped onion, and just a small piece of the chopped tomato. Fry until the onion softens. Note: Adding too much tomato at this stage releases moisture, which makes the bhindi slimy.
3. **Add Bhindi** - *1 min* - Toss the chopped bhindi into the pan and stir for 1 minute at 100°C.
4. **Season** - *2 mins* - Add salt, haldi, coriander powder, and red chili powder. Stir well to coat the bhindi evenly. Cook until the bhindi begins to look dry and the sliminess cooks off.
5. **Add Tomatoes and Finish** - *3 mins* - Place the remaining chopped tomatoes on top of the bhindi. Do not stir — just let them sit. Turn off the heat after 3 minutes.
6. **Steam** - *5 mins* - Cover the pan with a lid immediately after turning off the heat. Let it sit for 5 minutes to finish in its own steam before serving.


# Pasta
### Prep: 10 mins | Cook: 22 mins

## Ingredients
- 2 cups Pasta (Penne or Fusilli work best for this style)
- 1 Liter Water (for boiling)
- Extra Virgin Olive Oil (or any neutral oil)
- 1 Onion, finely chopped
- 1 Tomato, finely chopped
- Pasta sauce & Tomato ketchup (this gives the dish an Indian-style flavor — adjust to taste)
- Spices: Salt, Lal Mirch (red chili powder), Coriander leaves (Dhaniya), chopped

## Instructions
1. **Boil Water** - *5 mins* - Pour 1 liter of water into a large pot or cooker. Add a generous pinch of salt and bring to a boil at 180°C.
2. **Cook Pasta** - *10 mins* - Add 2 cups of pasta to the boiling salted water. Stir once immediately to prevent sticking, then lower to 130°C. Cook for about 10 minutes or follow the package instructions.
3. **Save Starch Water and Drain** - *2 mins* - Before draining, scoop out and save 1 cup of the starchy pasta water — this is key for making the sauce silky later. Drain the pasta and toss it with a tiny drizzle of oil to prevent sticking. Note: Do not rinse pasta with cold water, as this washes off the starch that helps the sauce cling.
4. **Make the Base** - *3-4 mins* - Heat olive oil in a pan at 100°C. Add the chopped onion, tomato, and dhaniya. Season with salt and lal mirch. Stir well, cover with a lid, and cook for 3 to 4 minutes.
5. **Simmer Sauce and Assemble** - *5 mins* - Add your pasta sauce and a squeeze of tomato ketchup to the pan and stir. Pour in up to 1 cup of the saved starchy pasta water to loosen the sauce. Toss the drained pasta into the pan and mix. Note: If the sauce looks too watery, cover with a lid for a few minutes at 100°C until it thickens and clings to the pasta.


# Egg Omelette
### Prep: 3 mins | Cook: 17 mins

## Ingredients
- 6 Eggs
- Spices of your choice (Salt, Pepper, Chili powder, etc.)
- Veggies of your choice (Onions, Chillies, Bell peppers, etc.), finely chopped
- 1 tbsp Butter or Oil

## Instructions
1. **Prep and Whisk** - *3 mins* - Crack 6 eggs into a large bowl. Add your finely chopped veggies and all your spices. Whisk thoroughly until the yolks and whites are fully combined and the mixture is slightly frothy.
2. **Slow Cook** - *13 mins* - Heat butter or oil in a wide frying pan at 130°C. Pour in the egg mixture. Do not stir — let it cook low and slow for 13 minutes. Note: This is a thick, frittata-style omelette due to the quantity. The slow low heat ensures the center cooks through without burning the bottom.
3. **Firm Up** - *4 mins* - Drop the temperature to 100°C. Let it cook for another 4 minutes until the center is fully set. Slide onto a plate and slice to serve.


# Cooked Quinoa
### Prep: 2-3 mins | Cook: 23 mins

## Ingredients
- 1.5 cups Quinoa
- 3 cups Water

## Instructions
1. **Wash the Quinoa** - *2-3 mins* - Rinse 1.5 cups of quinoa thoroughly under cold water to remove the bitter natural coating (saponin). Rub the grains between your palms as you rinse.
2. **Pressure Cook** - *7-8 mins* - Put the washed quinoa and 3 cups of water into the pressure cooker. Close the lid and pressure cook at 180°C for 7 to 8 minutes, then turn off the heat. Note: Quinoa expands significantly while cooking — 1.5 cups of dry quinoa yields a very large amount of cooked food, enough for several meals.
3. **Release Pressure and Fluff** - *10-15 mins* - Let the pressure cooker sit untouched for 10 to 15 minutes until the steam releases naturally. Open and fluff the quinoa with a fork before serving.
