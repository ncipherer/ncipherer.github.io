A running catalog of vivid dreams that shook me enough to wake up with a racing heart, audible laughs, or a sudden, violent return of consciousness.

## 1. The Stolen Bags & The Colony Chase

A woman steals my cloth bag and tosses it into the back of an auto. I keep asking her to return it — politely, then firmly — but she's the archetypal *tuntun mausi*: unmovable, unapologetic, utterly unbothered. Then a second woman emerges from a nearby house and joins her side. Together, they take my small trolley too, loading it in beside the bag.

Something snaps. I push past them, reach into the auto, and pull both bags out. Cloth bag in one hand, trolley tucked under the other arm, I run.

Behind me, the first woman starts shouting across the colony: *"Bhaag raha hai!"* Doors open. More women appear. I don't look back — I can't afford to. My lungs are already burning.

I make a turn. In that half-second, I glance back: they've rallied the whole lane and they've spotted me. Ahead, the main road stretches out — tempos and bikes moving steadily right to left. Two choices come to my mind: sprint for the road and try to flag something down before they catch up, or duck into the overgrown grass garden just around the corner and wait them out till nightfall.

I'm still deciding when the adrenaline jolts me awake.

## 2. The Stone Game & Manjulika

I'm inside a dream but not as myself — I'm occupying someone else's perspective, one of three guys sitting in a circle with a girl, playing a game that involves throwing stones at each other. It's rough, but lighthearted. One guy in the group takes every hit and never retaliates.

From inside my borrowed perspective, I nudge him: *throw one back*. He hesitates. The game continues. Occasionally the whole circle turns on my POV guy, and he responds by grabbing a fistful of stones and scattering them back.

Then the quiet guy picks up a stone much larger than the rest and hurls it at the girl.

Everyone freezes. She's still smiling — looking up — as a slow wash of blood runs down from her head, cutting clean lines through the dust on her face. Still smiling.

The group recovers fast. Someone cracks a joke about how she can't show up to a doctor like this. Someone else says she looks like SpongeBob. The mood, impossibly, stays light.

In the next scene, the girl and two of the guys have improvised a disguise: they're dressed like *Manjulika* from *Bhool Bhulaiyaa*, the blood doubling as her *sindoor*, limping through the neighbourhood in an exaggerated *Manthara* shuffle so no one recognizes them.

I woke up to the sound of my own laughter.

## 3. The 20-Year Sleep & Yakub

I'm on a train. Somewhere in the backstory of the dream — which arrives as felt memory rather than shown scenes — I've had to kill someone in self-defense during train journey.

A man I recognized from news coverage boards with a group and pulls me and my elder brother from our berths — me from the upper, my brother from the lower. What follows is a long, mocking interrogation: he doesn't believe my self-defense claim, and I keep arguing back. Just before they drop off the train, he shoots us both.

I fall to the side. Still alive. I keep my eyes shut, listening. Eventually I open them a fraction — my brother's legs are twitching, his chest still rising and falling. The station announcement crackles overhead. They've gone.

Then, without transition, I'm hearing a TV news broadcast: the reporter is describing what that man did *twenty years ago*, when he killed two brothers on a train.

I wake up in the train carriage. My brother is still here. We rush out. My arm — the side I was shot on — is throbbing, stiff with what feels like decades of disuse. We were lying there for twenty years. The arm I was shot on happens to be the exact side I am sleeping on in real life.

I run to my parents' room and tell them we're those brothers. We start looking up something called *"The Edge"* online — somehow the key to getting our story out. Then it hits me: if my arm is this damaged, I won't be able to do martial arts anymore. That thought is what finally unravels the dream. The boundary dissolves, and I'm awake.

My heartbeat is elevated. The aching side of my body is exactly where I was lying. Every detail is crystalline — which is how I know it'll be gone in minutes. I reach for my phone immediately to note this all down.
