A running catalog of vivid dreams that shook me enough to wake me up with a racing heart, audible laughs, or a sudden return of consciousness.

## 1. The Stolen Bags & The Colony Chase

A lady stole my cloth bag and put it in the back of an auto (inside it). I kept asking for it and for her to return, but she was a typical *tuntun mausi* kind of figure who would just keep it. And then suddenly another lady comes from inside a home and starts shouting at me too. Together, they take away my small trolley too and put it in the back of the auto as well.

At this point, I let go of my hesitation, push them a bit, and take both bags back from inside the auto. I take one cloth bag in my hand and hold the trolley in hand too (hands wrapped around it longitudinally) and run.

I start losing breath and she starts calling the entire colony to catch me, shouting *"Bhaag raha hai!"* I keep running with barely any breath without looking back.

The last scene is me making a turn and looking back—they're still gathering other women from nearby homes together, and they see me turn. After the turn, I see the main road in the distance with tempos and bikes passing from right to left.

I am left with a choice: make a run with whatever little life I have left and try to hitch a ride, or hide in the large grass garden in front of the home right at the turn and wait for people to assume I've escaped via the main road before making a run at night.

And in that rush of adrenaline and anxiety, I wake up.

## 2. The Stone Game & Manjulika

A few kids are playing a game where they throw stones at each other while sitting in a circle: one girl and three guys (I am experiencing this through the perspective of one of the guys).

One guy is just suffering and not hitting back. The guy whose perspective I am in encourages him to throw one too. He hesitates, and the usual stone throwing continues. Occasionally, everyone gangs up and throws at my POV guy, who retaliates by grabbing a fistful of stones to throw back.

Suddenly, the silent guy picks up a much bigger stone and hurls it at the girl. The POV guy tries to stop the silent guy, and everyone turns to look at the girl. She is smiling and looking up, and a sudden rush of blood comes down, washing the soil clean off her head—and she is still smiling.

The guys and girls start joking that she can't go to the doctor looking like this, and tease that she looks like SpongeBob.

In the next scene, two guys and the girl are dressed up like *Manjulika* from *Bhool Bhulaiyaa* (with the blood serving as the *sindoor* from the original getup), walking with a hilarious, exaggerated limp like *Manthara* so that no one in the neighbourhood recognizes them.

And I woke up to the sound of my own audible laughing.

## 3. The 20-Year Sleep & Yakub Memon

Sitting in a train. Had to kill people in self-defense.

Yakub Memon enters with his group and kidnaps me and my elder brother from our berths (I was on the upper berth, my brother on the lower). There is a long exchange: mocking me for claiming I was innocent and only acted in self-defense. I keep arguing back, but right before dropping off the train, he shoots both me and my brother.

I fall to the side, still alive, keeping my eyes shut and waiting for them to depart. I open my eyes slightly and see my brother's legs twitching, his stomach rising and falling as he breathes. I hear the railway announcement indicating that the train has stopped.

Suddenly, the next thing I hear is a TV news broadcast reporting what Yakub Memon did 20 years ago when he killed two brothers.

I suddenly wake up in the train and rush out after waking my brother up too. The arm and side I had fallen onto after getting shot are aching—naturally, because I had supposedly been lying on that side for 20 years (which happened to be the exact side I was sleeping on in real life).

I run into my parents' room and tell them that we are the brothers who were killed by Yakub Memon. We start searching for something called *"The Edge"* on the internet, which is somehow connected to getting our story out to the world.

Suddenly, it hits me: if my arm is hurting like this, I won't be able to practice martial arts anymore. And right then, the boundary between dream and reality begins to dissolve as I slowly wake up.

The aching part of my body is the exact side I was lying on while having the dream. My heartbeat is elevated, but gradually settling back to normal. Every detail of the dream is crystal clear in this moment—knowing it will start fading away within minutes, I immediately grab my phone to write it down before I forget.
