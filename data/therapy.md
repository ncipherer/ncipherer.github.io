I have undergone through therapy two times in my life. Both the times, I just felt very unprepared about the situation, and I wanted a external perspective about it.

Now, one thing about therapy, which I have opinion is that it's very important for you to put in efforts for it to work. You can keep pushing back on the illogical Ness of things, but then you are just wasting your time and your therapist time in my experience. The reason I'm saying this is because I want to understand how committed I was fixing things because the situation I was in just didn't seem make something I can continue at that time. 

And then I offer how my views have changed over the years.

## First time

I had just joined a great company, right out of college. And I was just killing it at work, my managers and my peers both enjoyed working with me. And I had a great interpersonal relationship and is genuinely be involved in maintaining those. I was leading some very high visibility and high impact projects, and had good engineering support from my TL.

But then Covid happened. And which distance this point where a lot of things started being very impersonal. The conversations about random things in life decreased. And somewhere the expectations of what is expected out of employee at work increased. The boundary between the work and Life were faint, and so I was working, maybe much more than what I was capable of maintaining in a longer term. On top of that came all the insecurity of leaders about people not doing work while they're working from home, and so the expectation to work even harder. And I felt like anything with anyone will ever give to me. I'll just say yes to it because saying no would be more detrimental to me and the interpersonal relationship that I wanted to maintain with this other person. And all of this high expectations from leadership, low number of boundaries from my side led to many debates with my manager about expectations from a person who is maybe an year out of university, I wasn't able to figure out how to navigate this.

I'll tell you an example. After three weeks of long night work to meet a deadline, because of a customer commit, I was just looking forward to a bit of a chill few weeks to recover mentally. But then suddenly, another big thing came up, and this was being made into an urgent thing, even though there was no reason for them to. And I said yes to that too. And while I was doing these long night continuous work, I realise that I am starting to get headaches and I'm starting to feel insomnia. And I was starting to get very anxious and irritat on small things. I was seeing this changing in me, just like my family was too, and I decided to bring it up in the internal team meetings. And all I got in response was after this, it will all be fine. Alert, it never got fine, even after that project was over. I was just consistently told that I have to adjust to the new normal.  So seeing that there is no alignment., I decided to just ask for a couple of days off (out of the ones I am already given on a yearly basis) to focus on my mental health. I posted a message on my team channel saying the same. And suddenly a lot of my peers started talking to me about how this is the right way to do things because we need to be proactive in these things and not reactive. Some of them even offered to have long distance, fun call about general Life stuff. But my manager took all differently, for him. This was someone showing the work culture problems inside the team to rest of the team. He kept asking me to edit my message to say differently. And that obviously led to a debate between us.

That point of me, not even knowing what to do if I am feeling overwhelmed was the trigger because of which I signed up for the therapy program that my company offered as a part of employee benefits. And I was genuinely determined to improve this situation because it was leading to a point where I was genuinely concerned about burning out and maybe leaving the job altogether.

No long story short the final conclusion of all those therapy sessions over the next 3 to 4 months was that I need to be more assertive because I have a very good sense of what is the right thing to do (yay integrity) but I just feel that I don't have the space to actually execute on those things because I'm constantly worried about impacting others. And so, I need to be more assertive in communicating my needs and boundaries.

Now all that worked very nicely for last five years or so. But recently I've been feeling a bit too assertive and rigid. And now that I am in a lead position, my words and what I say suddenly carry five times more weight than it did earlier. And that has led to some awkward conversations and some not so nice feedback. Fortunately, this time instead of going for therapy, I had a great mentor. And a single hour of discussion with him, gave me much more clarity as to how one needs to do based on the situation and what they need to do, and how to read a situation, then whatever months of therapy gave me. And I also have a much more solid understanding that this is not a thing which I can just change it over a couple of weeks, but rather something which will show up over 5-6 months and the speed at which I do, it has to be slow and that's the cracks of this entire situation. Now I have a roadmap of what to do for next couple of weeks, and then for next 4-5 months. And I am already working on it.


## second time

I will not be able to give much details about this one, unfortunately. This was when I started dating someone I had no voice for 5-6 years. And soon enough, when we started having some conversations about Life, I realise what a big gap we have in how we look at Life.


And there were some things which I realise were wrong with me as well. For example, my solution to any fight between us was to bring up some old fight and tell her how she always does that kind of thing. Or my solution to not understanding a situation in the heat of the moment was to ask her as many questions as I can about the situation, thinking that would help, and it was very selfish of me because I was seeking clarity, but I was overwhelming her.

There were quite a few things which I feel were very social media coded for her while I was trying to do a more realistic understanding. I look at honesty and emotional present in a relationship as a bare minimum, for her as long as the partner doesn't know the rest of the stuff it's alright. For me what you say is what you do was the main thing and I would be very particular about things like you said you would do this. Why did you not do that? And for her, it was just something that she thought about doing, and wasn't actually making a commitment or something that she wanted me to take on her word.

Anyways, I took therapy sessions again this time. And I discussed about all these different things I disagree with and how I feel I am told there is a certain way that I have to react to this situation by her, but it doesn't go with my way of handling situation. And discussion mostly relied on whatever sense of the world and how relationship should work I had formed over the years in my dating experiences, irrespective of how wrong for the other person they were. Because in a weight felt like I have hired this one therapist who is only concerned about I get what I want. I didn't feel like I have a therapist. It felt like I have an advocate fighting in a legal battle about some thing for me.

Anyways, long story, short, the relationship got ended soon after. And honestly, it was coming a long time. We both were just very very, very incompatible. So therapy was definitely not the reason why it ended.

But what I realised after a few months, thinking about all the therapy experience was how at no point I was told all the things I was doing wrong in that moment how I was being unfair to her. And his therapy was not able to offer me any perspective, and was only going to make me rely on whatever decision compass, I have formed over the years it's a few things for me personally.


## so does therapy work?

Based on my two experiences, it doesn't at least for me.

I don't shy away from challenging my own ideas and conventional wisdom, if presented a new perspective with. So this whole CHARADE of therapy is just there to guide and help you untangle your own thoughts, doesn't really work for me.
I do want someone to tell me if I am doing wrong thing so that I can change it. And if you can't do that, then at least don't encourage me how I am right. Because all the then providing me is a echo chamber. And I don't use social media for running away from an echo chamber only, at least don't make one when I'm paying for it haha

I feel for me at least therapy is a good option. If you want very short term, answer to a problem that you have no clue on how to deal with because you feel like you have no tools right now. But if I had to do it all over again, I would just find a mentor who has either gone through a similar situation or has hepld people through the situation and at least can offer me a candid and honest response even if that means asking, are you fucking stupid?

And I'm glad that I have over the last year, also so managed to figure out how to find these mentors, and I have a good network of people that I can now lean on if I am going through such situations again.

A good analogy to how therapy worked in my end, my case only personally - would be fascism, it can offer a good solution when there is no solution in a country for a situation. But the country needs to move away from that once it is no longer in the danger of that problem, and move back to an equitable and democratic form of governance. Singapore is one such fantastic example.

And so even though they were very useful in the time when I didn't had any solution, now I need to spend time in reversing them just so that I can continue to live a happy life.
