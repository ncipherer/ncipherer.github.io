> I was reading a very old journal entry of mine and realised how frequently I have changed my personality, and even my opinions about things I once believed whole-heartedly. Sometimes it happened over time, with experience. Other times, someone who cared about me fought me for it. So this isn't a list of my problems - it's a record of how they change, and of the trajectory I am on. That's why it stays a WIP: I keep catching new things in myself, and there's no version of it where the list is finished. I'll keep coming back and adding them as I find them :)

## Righteous

I believed there's only one way to live the life, the right way. It was easy to cloak that under "I have high standards", except the standards were just what was convenient to me. Part of it was self-esteem: it felt good, because it meant I was better than others.

The same standards also run on me - I move the goal posts every time I get close to them. I am still easing around the idea that improvement cannot be the entire project. At some point you have to become someone you are allowed to enjoy being, and be proud of who you are - present tense.

## Empathy

I thought I was good at empathy, because strangers were easy - cab drivers, random train passengers, anyone. But strangers don't carry my history, activate my wounds, or mirror back the parts of myself I have avoided for years. The people closest to me do all three.

Graciousness saved for strangers isn't kindness, it is image management. The measure isn't how I treat someone who can't hurt me; it is the tone I use with the people who know me best.

At work I come across as assertive and overpowering, to the point where people would rather avoid the awkward conversation than have it with me. So the practice is deliberate - fake the empathy, praise people before I give them feedback, then slowly stop faking it and actually feel from their side.

## Disagreements

Disagreeing right takes every little ounce of courage and empathy. Courage to consider whether I am the volatile one. Empathy to figure out the other person's negotiables and non-negotiables. I'm still learning to convey my feelings, and to take a break when I feel flooded with emotions.

The tendency is to drop out of my heart and into my head to solve problems, so much conflict used to be me negotiating the best way back to "safety" - to the place where I feel good. Over time I have started to realise how easy it is to forget that the goal was always to spend as much time heart-centered as possible, doing things that inspire the heart. So I'm beginning to leave my brain out of disagreements.

## Patience

Whenever I start anything - from training to relationships - my immediate impulse is to check whether I'm comfortable with its hardest level. That may work with inanimate objects, but not with people. Because progress on a hobby is easy to track. But people? I used to have a constant need for reassurance that I was doing my best by their standards. If I don't get a continuous supply of those affirmations, my anxiety takes over, discipline and patience go to trash, and every disagreement becomes urgent enough to need a conversation. So I have started treating patience as my only answer.

## Maintenance

I don't spend time maintaining relationships. That's the plainest way to say it. I show up fully for someone when I am in front of them, and give them almost nothing when I am not - no calls, no check-ins, no "how have you been." I call that just who I am; it is really a choice I make with every new person I meet.

The friendships that made this obvious are ones I outgrew. They weren't unlearning the shit we had been conditioned to; they were flexing their ignorance instead. So I stopped showing up. No fight, no conversation, just a slow fade. A relationship I don't feed isn't a relationship, it's a memory - and I have more of those than I want to admit.

## Silence

There is a second half to how I treat the people close to me: my wins, I share immediately. My problems, I keep.

It isn't an accident - it's a deliberate choice, and I have my reasons for it.

One, anything between you and me I will say to your face, because it affects us. Anything else going on in my head stays mine.

Two, I have always landed on my feet even from things which seem like the end of the world, so I don't see why I should make you anxious about me.

Three, I compartmentalise well, and the people close to me are my safe space - the one place I want to keep free of everything else I am going through.

This isn't me closing the door on anyone. It just takes me months to be comfortable telling the story. But I do tell them eventually.
