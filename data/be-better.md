> I was reading a very old journal entry and realised how much I've changed - my personality, opinions, things I once believed completely. Sometimes it happened slowly with experience. Other times someone who cared about me literally fought me on it. So this isn't a list of my problems. It's just me tracking how things shift, and where I'm headed. I keep adding to it as I notice new things. There's no finished version of this.

## Righteous

For a long time, having strong opinions about how things should be done felt like a good thing. It kept me consistent, gave me a direction. I wasn't just floating around - I had a way of doing things. And I still think some of that is fine. The issue was that I built those standards around what worked for me specifically, and then acted like they applied to everyone. And I ran them on myself just as hard - every time I got close to what I wanted, I'd move the bar. Kept me going, but also meant I never actually landed anywhere.

I still believe in standards. I just don't want to carry them around like a verdict on everything and everyone. I'm trying to get to a place where I can also just be okay with where I am right now - not always in the middle of fixing something.

## Empathy

I thought I was decent at empathy. Strangers were easy - I was curious, patient, not judgy. That part was real. But strangers don't know me. They don't bring up old stuff or reflect back the parts of me I've been avoiding. The people closest to me do, and that's where I'd run out of patience.

Being warm to people who can't affect me isn't empathy, it's just easy. The actual test is how I treat people who know me well. At work people have told me I can be hard to approach. I don't think that's who I am at my core, but I've had to accept it's what they experience. So I've been making a deliberate effort - slow down, ask first, make room. The hope is for it to be getting less deliberate over time.

## Disagreements

Disagreeing right takes every little ounce of courage and empathy. Courage to consider whether I am the volatile one. Empathy to figure out the other person's negotiables and non-negotiables. I'm still learning to convey my feelings, and to take a break when I feel flooded with emotions.

What I'm slowly getting is that staying connected during a disagreement is the whole point - not resolving it cleanly. I still think being honest and direct matters. I just also think that going cold and logical in the middle of a fight is usually just me checking out emotionally. I'm working on staying in it.

## Patience

With anything new I want to understand the hardest version first before I commit. For skills that's fine. For people, it makes no sense - there's no ceiling to test, progress isn't linear, and you can't see it in real time. So without visible progress I'd start reaching for reassurance. Constant check-ins on whether I was doing okay by someone's standards. And if that didn't come, the anxiety would fill in the gaps and everything would start feeling urgent.

Wanting to know where you stand isn't the problem. How I responded when I didn't have that answer was. I've started treating patience like something I actually have to pick up and use rather than waiting for it to just show up. And I've gotten better at trusting what's already there instead of needing it reconfirmed all the time.

## Maintenance

When I'm with someone I'm fully there. But in between? Nothing. No check-in texts, no random calls, no "hey how are you doing." I used to say that's just how I'm wired - depth over frequency. There's truth in that. But it was also just a convenient way to describe a choice I kept making without really examining it.

The friendships that made this obvious are ones I outgrew. They weren't unlearning the shit we had been conditioned to; they were flexing their ignorance instead. So I stopped showing up. No fight, no conversation, just a slow fade. A relationship I don't feed isn't a relationship, it's a memory - and I have more of those than I want to admit.

## Silence

My wins I share immediately. My problems I keep to myself.

That's a deliberate choice. Anything that's between me and someone else I'll say directly because it involves them. Everything else I carry alone - I almost always figure it out eventually, and the people I love are the one space I want to keep separate from everything I'm going through. That space matters to me.

But I've also been running a quieter story underneath that: that the people I love already have enough on their plate - building a life, growing a family, their own weight - so mine doesn't really count. And the same people keep asking me to share because they actually want to hear it, which kind of makes the excuse fall apart. I wasn't protecting them. I was just deciding on their behalf that they couldn't hold it, which isn't fair to them or to me.

I'm still working on how to honour both these opinions within me.
